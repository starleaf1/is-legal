# Case Record 2022-031: Payments to Expedite Customs Clearance

*Sample document for the Is Legal demo. Fictional.*

Declassified case summary, approved for internal training use by the Ethics & Compliance Office on 3 April 2023. Names have been removed.

## Background

A regional supply chain manager was under pressure to release a delayed shipment of spare parts held at a Northland port. A customs officer suggested the shipment could be cleared "today instead of next week" for a cash payment of NLD 400, described as an overtime fee, for which no official receipt would be issued. The manager paid the amount personally and claimed it back as "port handling" on an expense report. The same arrangement was repeated on three later occasions through a customs broker engaged by Acme, totalling around NLD 1,900.

## Findings

1. The payments were facilitation payments, prohibited by section 5 of the Code of Conduct, and offences under Article 6(1) of the Northland Anti-Bribery and Corruption Act 2019, which has no exception for small payments to speed up routine government action.
2. The manager did not face any threat to personal safety, so the duress exception in Article 6(2) and in the Code did not apply.
3. Recording the payments as "port handling" was a false entry in Acme's books and records.
4. Routing later payments through the customs broker did not change their nature; the Code prohibits using third parties to do what employees may not do themselves. It also exposed Acme to liability under Article 7 of the Act for failing to prevent bribery.

## Outcome

- The manager's employment was terminated.
- Acme self-reported the payments to the Northland Anti-Corruption Commission under Article 10 of the Act and cooperated with its inquiry. The Commission did not prosecute Acme, citing the prompt self-report and Acme's compliance program.
- The customs broker's contract was terminated, and all brokers were required to re-certify compliance with Acme's anti-bribery requirements.

## Lessons for employees

- Refuse any request for an unofficial payment to speed up a government process, however small or customary it seems, and report it to the Ethics & Compliance Office.
- Official, published expedited-service fees paid to a government agency against an official receipt are not facilitation payments. If you're unsure which you're being asked for, ask Legal before paying.
- Never disguise a payment in expense reports or invoices.
