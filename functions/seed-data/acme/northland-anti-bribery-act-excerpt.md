# Northland Anti-Bribery and Corruption Act 2019 (Excerpt)

*Sample document for the Is Legal demo. "Northland" is a fictional country and this Act is invented.*

Enacted by the Parliament of Northland. In force from 1 July 2019. Administered by the Northland Anti-Corruption Commission.

Article 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Anti-Bribery and Corruption Act 2019.

Article 2 Definitions

In this Act:

"advantage" means anything of value, financial or otherwise, including money, gifts, loans, fees, rewards, employment, services, favors and hospitality, whether given directly or through a third party.

"public official" means any person holding a legislative, administrative or judicial office in Northland or any other country, whether appointed or elected; any person performing a public function, including an employee of a government agency or a state-owned enterprise, a customs or tax officer, or an inspector; and any official of a public international organization.

"commercial organisation" means a company or partnership, wherever incorporated, that carries on business or part of a business in Northland.

Article 3 Bribing another person

A person commits an offence if they offer, promise or give an advantage to another person intending to induce that person to perform a function improperly, or to reward improper performance.

Article 4 Being bribed

A person commits an offence if they request, agree to receive or accept an advantage intending that a function will be performed improperly as a result, whether by themselves or another person.

Article 5 Bribery of public officials

A person commits an offence if they offer, promise or give any advantage to a public official, directly or through a third party, with the intention of influencing the official in their official capacity in order to obtain or retain business or an advantage in the conduct of business. It is not necessary to prove that the official acted improperly.

Article 6 Facilitation payments

(1) There is no exception in this Act for facilitation payments. A payment or other advantage given to a public official to expedite or secure the performance of a routine governmental action, including processing permits, visas or customs clearance, is an offence under Article 5 regardless of its amount.

(2) A person does not commit an offence under paragraph (1) if they made the payment because they reasonably believed there was an imminent threat to their life, health or liberty, provided they report the payment to the Commission within 7 days.

Article 7 Failure of commercial organisations to prevent bribery

(1) A commercial organisation commits an offence if a person associated with it, including an employee, agent or subsidiary, bribes another person intending to obtain or retain business or an advantage for the organisation.

(2) It is a defence for the organisation to prove that it had adequate procedures in place designed to prevent persons associated with it from committing bribery.

Article 8 Hospitality and promotional expenditure

Bona fide hospitality or promotional expenditure that is reasonable and proportionate, made in good faith to present products or services or to build business relationships, is not of itself an offence. The more lavish the expenditure, and the closer it is in time to a decision the recipient will make, the more likely it is to be treated as intended to influence that decision.

Article 9 Penalties

(1) An individual guilty of an offence under Articles 3 to 6 is liable to imprisonment for up to 10 years, a fine of up to NLD 2,000,000, or both.

(2) A commercial organisation guilty of an offence under Article 5 or 7 is liable to an unlimited fine and may be excluded from public procurement for up to 5 years.

Article 10 Self-reporting and cooperation

The Commission shall take into account, in deciding whether to prosecute and in recommending penalties, whether the organisation reported the conduct promptly and voluntarily and cooperated fully with the investigation.
